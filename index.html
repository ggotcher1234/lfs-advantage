Timing creates the opening

Recognize the change that opens a sales window.

When business is stable, buyers often have little reason to risk changing suppliers.

An acquisition, new leadership, product launch, expansion, or emerging business problem can change those priorities—and create an opening to consider alternatives.

  1. 01 / STABILITY

    The current approach works.

    No pressing reason to reconsider suppliers.

  2. 02 / CHANGE

    Something shifts.

    A new project, investment, leader, requirement, or problem creates questions.

  3. 03 / INVESTIGATION

    The sales window may open.

    Buyers explore ways forward. Relevant expertise can earn consideration.

    A timely reason to engage
  4. 04 / SELECTION

    Choices become commitments.

    Suppliers are evaluated and selected. Confirm what remains open.

A signal is a reason to investigate. A conversation confirms whether the sales window is open.

A business reason to call

Show that you understand what is changing.

The familiar pitch

“We make watches.
You need to know the time.”

“We provide equipment and software for energy companies. Can we schedule a meeting?”

The buyer has to work out why the conversation matters now.

The informed approach

“I noticed your project just received funding.”

“I saw that you secured funding for the refinery project. We provide controls and monitoring tools for projects like this. Is that part of the project still being evaluated?”

A specific development. A relevant capability. A useful question.

Illustrative outreach. Relevance creates a stronger opening; your salesperson still earns the next step.
